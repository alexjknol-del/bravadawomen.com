Lubricant Basics: What Actually Matters

Lubricant is one of the simplest, most effective upgrades to comfort during sex, yet it still carries an odd stigma — as if needing it says something about you or your partner. It doesn't. Natural lubrication varies by hormone levels, medication, stress, and time of the cycle, none of which are within your control.

Water-based lubricants are the most broadly compatible option: safe with condoms, safe with silicone toys, and easy to wash off fabric. Silicone-based lubricants last longer and feel silkier but shouldn't be used with silicone toys, since they can degrade the material over time.

Ingredient lists deserve a closer look than most people give them. Glycerin and added sugars can disrupt the vaginal microbiome and contribute to yeast infections in sensitive users. Parabens and synthetic fragrances are common irritants worth avoiding if you've had reactions before.

Hyaluronic acid has become a popular addition to water-based lubricants for the same reason it shows up in skincare: it holds onto moisture and helps the lubricant feel less like it needs constant reapplication.

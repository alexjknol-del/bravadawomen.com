Pelvic Floor Strength & Pain

The pelvic floor is a group of muscles most women never think about until something goes wrong: leaking when you sneeze, discomfort during sex, or a persistent ache that's hard to place. Both a weak pelvic floor and an overly tense one can cause these symptoms, which is why "just do Kegels" isn't always the right advice.

Pregnancy, childbirth, menopause, and even high-impact exercise over years can all weaken pelvic floor muscles. Symptoms include mild urinary leakage, reduced sensation, or a feeling of heaviness — all worth addressing rather than accepting as normal.

On the other end, a chronically tense pelvic floor — often linked to stress, prior pain, or bracing habits — can cause pain during penetration or intercourse. This calls for relaxation and release work, not strengthening, so an accurate read on which issue you're dealing with matters.

Pelvic floor trainers, whether app-connected biofeedback devices or simple weighted tools, help by making an invisible muscle group visible: you can see or feel whether you're actually engaging the right muscles, rather than guessing. Consistency over a few months tends to matter more than intensity.
